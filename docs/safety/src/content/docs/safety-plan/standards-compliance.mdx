This chapter names the standard Slint SC targets, and the integrity level Slint SC is developed for.
The [Norm Mapping](/norm-mapping/) maps each requirement of ISO 26262 to the part of this manual that meets it.

## Standard in Scope

Slint SC targets **ISO 26262:2018** at **ASIL B**.
Every clause number in this manual refers to that edition.

Nothing in this manual is specific to one application domain.

## Safety Requirements

<Addresses>ISO 26262-8 Clause 6</Addresses>

The standard tells us what properties a safety-critical system must have, such as traceability, freedom from interference, and determinism.
The standard doesn't tell us how to write requirements with those properties for a GUI toolkit.

Slint SC states its safety requirements on three levels.
The [Assumed Safety Requirements](/component-documentation/assumed-safety-requirements/) say what an integrator needs from Slint SC, derived from its assumed use.
The [Language Specification](/reference/language/) says what the language and the runtime must do, one identified paragraph at a time.
Each paragraph is traced to the tests that verify the paragraph.
The [Tool Evaluation Report](/evaluation-report/potential-errors/) and the runtime's [Safety Analysis](/component-documentation/safety-analysis/) instead say what can go wrong.
For each potential error, both documents name the measure that detects or constrains the error.

## Integrity Level

Slint SC is a Safety Element out of Context (SEooC).
That's the ISO 26262 term for a component developed before anyone knows which product it goes into.
Without a product there are no hazards to rate, and no HARA (Hazard Analysis and Risk Assessment) to derive an ASIL from.
So Slint SC has no integrity level of its own.
Slint SC is developed to be capable of ASIL B, so ASIL B is a target rather than a result.
The integrator confirms that ASIL B is the level their application needs.

ASIL (Automotive Safety Integrity Level) describes the risk level of something.
ASIL D is the highest level, C is high, B is medium, and A is low; QM means not safety critical.

Where an application isn't safety related at all, the integrator can use the full Slint framework instead, and none of this manual applies.

## Tailoring

<Addresses>ISO 26262-2 6.4.5</Addresses>

### Impact Analysis

ISO 26262-2 6.4.3 asks for an impact analysis at the item level: an analysis of whether a vehicle system is new or changed.
Slint SC isn't part of a specific vehicle system, so the item-level impact analysis doesn't apply.

Slint SC reuses the Slint compiler, which wasn't developed to ISO 26262.
The compiler is a tool, not an element of the product, so ISO 26262-8 Clause 11 covers it in the [Tool Evaluation Report](/evaluation-report/).

Each change to Slint SC runs through the change impact analysis of ISO 26262-8 Clause 8 instead; see [Development Phases](/safety-plan/development-phases/).

### Safety Element out of Context

ISO 26262-2 6.4.5.7 lets an SEooC tailor the lifecycle, provided that:

1. the component's requirements are derived from assumptions about the component's intended use and context, and
2. the integrator validates those assumptions in their target application.

Slint SC meets (1) with its [Assumed Safety Requirements](/component-documentation/assumed-safety-requirements/) and the [Assumptions of Use](/user-manual/#assumptions-of-use).
The User Manual hands (2) to the integrator.

### Parts of the Lifecycle

Parts 2, 6, and 8 of ISO 26262 apply to Slint SC, except where a row below says otherwise.
[Norm Mapping](/norm-mapping/) maps their requirements one by one.

Each row below names a part of the reference lifecycle that Slint SC omits or performs differently, or that isn't in Parts 2, 6, and 8.
